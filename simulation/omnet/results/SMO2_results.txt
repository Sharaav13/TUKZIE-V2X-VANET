==========================================================================================================================
TVV (TUKZIE V2X VANET) - SM/O2 - BACK-OFF SWEEP (best back-off)
==========================================================================================================================
Simulation test SM/O2 of the report (Methodology): PDR, collisions, access delay, transmission interval,
end-to-end delay and peer table of a 10-node TVV cluster for a maximum back-off of 0, 1.5, 3, 6 and 12 s,
on the TVV OMNeT++ model after calibrating its channel to the field tests (tests/analysis.txt).

Contents: 1 calibration, 2 configuration, 3 results, 4 interpretation, 5 limitations.


1. CHANNEL CALIBRATION: HOW tests/analysis.txt WAS USED
==========================================================================================================================

(This section is the same in SMO1_results.txt and SMO2_results.txt.)

1.1 Starting point (before)
---------------------------
The lora-v2v [General] channel (omnetpp.ini) was INET LogNormalShadowing with alpha = 3.5, sigma = 6 dB, referenced
to free space at 1 m (25.18 dB at 433.2 MHz), isotropic antennas, no system loss, and FLoRa's receiver (decodes a
frame received above its sensitivity table, -137 dBm at SF12/125 kHz, unless it collides). With 10 dBm that puts the
50 % PDR point at about 3 km (two-node sweep, section 1.6). The real nodes lost contact at about 324 m.

1.2 Measurements used
---------------------
Only the UCT RUGBY FIELD section of tests/analysis.txt (open field, nodes stationary at GPS-measured positions):

  Run      PDR A->B   PDR B->A   Mean distance   Max received at
  run 1      2.9 %     32.8 %       200.1 m          288.4 m
  run 2      0.0 %     48.2 %       222.6 m          302.9 m
  run 3      6.2 %     37.1 %       252.1 m          324.0 m
  Overall    3.3 %     38.5 %       224.2 m          324.0 m
  RSSI fit: RSSI = 37.09 e^(-0.05055 d) - 117.3 dBm      SNR fit: SNR = 31.59 e^(-0.01471 d) - 17.45 dB
  PDR fit (both directions, 25-packet bins): PDR = 0.0009184 d^2 - 0.5476 d + 102.4 %

The fits in analysis.txt pool both directions and only see received packets, so instead the channel was fitted to
the raw per-packet logs behind them (tests/uct rugby field/run 1..3, vanet_log<n>.txt with the receivers' full
logs and sensor_log<n>.txt GPS), extracted exactly as tests/test_analysis.m does (a sent beacon is matched to the
receiver's RX line by seq; distance = sender's position in the packet to the receiver's nearest GPS fix, <= 5 s
apart). The extraction reproduces analysis.txt (Bob -> Alice 62/189, 66/137, 63/170; Alice -> Bob 1/35, 0/23,
2/32).

  * Direction: Bob -> Alice only. Alice -> Bob delivered 3 of 90 beacons (3.3 %, none beyond 45 m) against 38.5 %
    the other way, so Alice's transmit path is faulty and does not represent a typical node.
  * Half duplex: 76 of Bob's 496 beacons overlapped one of Alice's own transmissions (PPS-timed TX start + time on
    air on both nodes); only 3 of those were received. They were removed from the channel fit (lost for a MAC
    reason, not a channel one), leaving 420 beacons, 188 received (44.8 %), at 1 to 332.4 m.
  * Noise floor and received power: the RSSI fit's asymptote, -117.3 dBm, equals the 125 kHz thermal floor plus a
    ~6 dB noise figure (-174 + 10 log10(125e3) + 6 = -117.0 dBm): beyond ~50 m the reported pktRssi is noise. The
    received signal power was therefore taken as Pr = -117.3 dBm + SNR for every received packet.
  * Link budget: Tx 10 dBm, 2 dBi antenna at each end, 0.14 dB cable loss at each end: PL(d) = 13.72 dBm - Pr(d).

1.3 Fit
-------
A straight regression of Pr on log10(d) over received packets is biased at range: packets below the SF12
demodulation limit (about -20 dB SNR) are never logged, so the far points over-represent lucky fades (a quick fit of
the SNR curve gave PL = 100.2 + 19.8 log10 d, i.e. alpha ~ 2). Instead a censored (Tobit-type) maximum-likelihood
fit was made over all 420 beacons, received and lost:

  Pr(d) ~ Normal(P0 - 10 alpha log10(d / 1 m), sigma)               log-distance path loss + log-normal shadowing
  P(decoded | Pr) = Phi((Pr - S) / tau)                              soft demodulation threshold S
  received beacon:  density of its Pr x P(decoded | Pr)   (SNR >= 10 dB treated as ">= Pr", the SX127x saturates)
  lost beacon:      P(not decoded) = Phi((S - mu(d)) / sqrt(sigma^2 + tau^2))

Result (all four channel parameters and the threshold free):

  P0 (Pr at 1 m)            -73.4 dBm      ->  PL(d) = 87.2 + 27.7 log10(d) dB (with antennas and cables)
  alpha                      2.77          (likelihood profile: alpha = 2.0 is worse by 23.4 log-units, 2.5 by 2.5,
                                            3.0 by 1.6, 3.5 by 12.5: approx. 95 % interval 2.55 to 3.05)
  sigma (residual spread)    5.8 dB
  S (demodulation limit)    -137.1 dBm     = SNR -19.8 dB over the -117.3 dBm floor
  tau (threshold softness)   0.9 dB        (small: the threshold is effectively hard)

  The quick estimate PL = 100.2 + 19.8 log10 d agrees at 100 m (139.8 vs 142.5 dB) but under-predicts the loss at
  300 m by 6.5 dB, as expected from the truncation. The ~62-77 dB of excess loss over free space at 1-300 m is the
  ground-level (antennas ~1 m, carried) open-field link; the fit is only meant for 10-330 m, not near 1 m.

1.4 Values put in the model (simulation/omnet/simulations/tvv.ini, [Config TvvFirmware], inherited by every Tvv config)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  *.LoRaMedium.pathLoss.typename      LogNormalShadowing (unchanged)
  *.LoRaMedium.pathLoss.alpha         3.5   ->  2.77
  *.LoRaMedium.pathLoss.sigma         6 dB  ->  5.8 dB
  *.LoRaMedium.pathLoss.systemLoss    0 dB  ->  58.3 dB
      The model's antennas are isotropic with no cable loss, so the 2 x 2 dBi antennas and 2 x 0.14 dB cables are
      folded in: systemLoss = 10 dBm - P0 - 25.18 dB (free space at 1 m, 433.2 MHz) = 10 + 73.4 - 25.2 = 58.3 dB.
  Receiver sensitivity: FLoRa's SF12/125 kHz table value, -137 dBm, kept. It is what the fit found on its own
      (-137.1 dBm), and the SX1278 datasheet figure; no change needed.
  Noise floor: FLoRa uses the same -137 dBm as the noise in its SNIR, 19.7 dB below the real -117.3 dBm floor.
      FLoRa never uses SNIR for the decode decision (only sensitivity and the 1 dB co-SF capture rule), so the
      receptions are right, but the model's rxSnr reads ~20 dB high (node SNR ~ rxPower + 117.3 dB). Not changed.
  Energy detection: -120 dBm ([General]) is only used by carrierSense = "energy" (TvvFirmwareEnergySense, not the
      node); the firmware's preamble carrier sense uses the receiver's RECEIVING state, i.e. the -137 dBm
      sensitivity. Not changed and not used in these sweeps.
  routing.rxProcessingDelay = 25.5 ms (new parameter, added to the end-to-end delay statistics): the PPS-timed
      Howard Centre packets give latency minus time on air of 24.36-25.00 ms (avg 24.64 ms, 7 packets) and
      27.52-27.53 ms (avg 27.53 ms, 3 packets); the packet-weighted mean is 25.5 ms. It is the node's processing
      between TxDone/RxDone and the timestamp, not channel time, so it does not change any MAC timing.

1.5 Other checks against tests/analysis.txt
-------------------------------------------
  * Time on air: the model's LoRaV2VTransmitter uses the Semtech formula for the 25-byte frame (24 B TvvPacket + the
    1 B header standing for dest), SF12, BW125, CR4/5, 8-symbol preamble, explicit header, CRC on, low data rate
    optimisation on: preamble (8 + 4.25) x 32.768 ms = 401.4 ms, payload 33 symbols = 1081.3 ms, total 1482.75 ms
    = the theoretical 1482.752 ms. The nodes measured 1482.979-1482.995 ms (TX start to TxDone), 0.23-0.24 ms
    (0.016 %) longer, which is interrupt/timer latency on the node, not airtime. Confirmed: no change. The model's
    direct end-to-end delay is 1482.75 + 25.5 = 1508.25 ms; measured TX start to RxDone 1507.34-1510.52 ms.
  * Forwarding (SHD TEST): every anticipated forward was sent, exactly once, with ttl 1 (Alice's beacons forwarded
    by Bob 7/7, Bob's by Alice 12/12, no unexpected forwards), and peer lists matched the RX lines. That is what
    TvvRouting does (a packet addressed to another node is forwarded once with ttl - 1; broadcasts are not), so the
    routing model needed no change.
  * Not used: HOWARD CENTRE (indoor/undercover, multi-storey building), MENZIES STAIRCASE (through floors), TUKZIE
    TEST and GW PARKING LOT (moving vehicles, terrain detection) and HELDERSIG STRAIGHT (GPS speed accuracy) are
    not open-area stationary link measurements, so they are not relevant to these open-area cluster sweeps (only
    Howard Centre's PPS latency was used, for the processing offset).
  * Observed while extracting: in the rugby field runs Bob transmitted every 3.48 s (2 s interval + airtime) while
    Alice transmitted every 16.5-20 s (15 s interval + airtime + back-off): the two nodes were running different
    BEACON_INTVL settings. This does not affect the per-packet channel fit.

1.7 Cluster radius chosen from the calibration
----------------------------------------------
Both sweeps put the nodes in a disc of radius 25 m, so the largest separation is 50 m, where the calibrated
two-node PDR is 99.8 %: every node hears every other, and the PDR losses in the sweeps come from the MAC
(collisions, half duplex, busy receivers), not from range.


2. CONFIGURATION
==========================================================================================================================

Test SM/O2, back-off sweep (purpose: the best back-off for a cluster). Configs in simulation/omnet/simulations/tvv.ini:
  SMO2             10 nodes, BACKOFF_MAX_MS = 0, 1500, 3000, 6000 (firmware), 12000 ms (BACKOFF_MIN_MS 0)
  SMO2CsDetect     sensitivity: SMO2 with carrier sense seeing a frame only after 4 preamble symbols (131 ms)
                   instead of at once (mac.csDetectionTime = 131ms; see section 5)
Both extend TvvCluster -> TvvStatic -> TvvFirmware. B = 6000 ms is the same configuration as SM/O1 at N = 10
(different seeds; the results agree within their CIs).

Model and tools
  * TVV model: simulation/omnet, formerly firmware/sim (TvvMac, TvvRouting, TvvNode, simulations/TvvStatic.ned, simulations/tvv.ini) at repo
    commit 139ea7a plus this session's changes to the model (calibrated channel, stationary network,
    traffic mix, warm-up-aware statistics, csDetectionTime), copied into ~/TVV/lora-v2v (WSL) and built
    MODE=release. OMNeT++ 6.1, INET 4.5, FLoRa 1.2.0 (LoRaMedium, LoRaRadio, LoRaV2VReceiver/Transmitter);
    Veins/SUMO not used (stationary nodes). Runs made 2026-10-25.
  * Tables: simulation/omnet/simulations/tvvstats.py (results/<config>-*.sca -> mean ± 95 % CI over seeds).
  * Reproduce: see simulation/omnet/README.md, "Stationary configs (no SUMO)".

Firmware parameters (system.h values, unchanged unless swept)
  LoRa 433.2125 MHz, SF12, BW 125 kHz, CR 4/5, 10 dBm, 8-symbol preamble, explicit header, CRC, LDRO;
  25-byte frame, 1482.75 ms on air. BEACON_INTVL 15 s from the end of the previous Tx, shared by beacons and
  forwards (a queued forward takes the slot). Back-off random(0, BACKOFF_MAX_MS) ms, restarted on every RxDone
  (also CRC errors); BACKOFF_MAX_MS 6000. Carrier sense: no Tx while a LoRa frame is being received (preamble
  sense). MAX_PEERS 3, PEER_TIMEOUT 30 s (checked every 1 s), DUP_WINDOW 90 s / DUP_HISTORY 48, TTL_INIT 2,
  FWD_QUEUE_SIZE 2. Channel: calibrated (section 1.4). rxProcessingDelay 25.5 ms added to the delay statistics.

Node layout
  N nodes in a disc of radius 25 m (largest separation 50 m: calibrated PDR 99.8 %, so every node hears every
  other), deterministic sunflower pattern: node i at r = 25 m x sqrt((i + 0.5)/N), angle i x 137.508 deg,
  centred at (1350 m, 1550 m), height 0 m, stationary (INET StationaryMobility). Network TvvStatic, no buildings.

Traffic
  Every node beacons whenever its slot allows (the firmware always has a beacon to send). Each beacon is
  addressed to a uniformly random other node with probability 0.1, otherwise broadcast
  (routing.addressedFraction = 0.1: the report's 90 % broadcast / 10 % addressed assumption; no fleet data exists).
  Every other node that hears an addressed beacon forwards it once (ttl 2 -> 1), as in the firmware and the SHD test.

Seeds, time
  10 repetitions per value (seed sets = OMNeT++ run numbers, all different), 3600 s simulated, the first 300 s
  discarded (warmup-period), so 3300 s measured per run (~100-220 transmissions per node). "/ h" figures are
  per hour of the measured period. Mean ± 95 % confidence interval over the 10 runs (Student t, 9 d.o.f.); each
  run's value is first averaged (or summed) over its nodes. CI 0.000 = identical in every run.

Metric definitions
  PDR per receiver        frames heard straight from their sender (any dest) / (heard + gaps in each sender's
                          seq), per receiving node, then averaged over nodes ("worst receiver": the lowest node).
  Frames lost             all receivers' seq gaps / expected frames.
  CRC errors              receptions that started (receiver locked on) but ended with a CRC error: the
                          collisions the node itself would see as RxDone with a bad CRC. Frames lost because the
                          receiver was transmitting (half duplex) or already locked onto another frame are lost
                          silently: they are in "Frames lost" but not here.
  Channel access delay    from the slot becoming due (15 s after the end of the previous Tx) to Tx start
                          (waiting for the back-off and for the channel); mean over all transmissions, and max.
  Tx interval             between consecutive Tx starts of a node (beacons and forwards); 16.48 s minimum.
  Own beacon interval     measured period x N / own beacons sent (forwards take slots from beacons).
  Forwards, % of frames   share of transmissions that were forwards.
  E2E delay direct        original Tx start to RxDone + 25.5 ms, frames heard directly.
  E2E delay forwarded     same, for every forwarded copy that reaches the node it is addressed to (before the
    copy                  repeat check), i.e. the delay of the two-hop path; mean and max.
  Forwarded copies at     forwarded copies reaching the destination per addressed beacon sent.
    dest / addressed pkt
  Delivered first via     addressed packets whose first copy at the destination was a forward (direct lost).
    forward
  Addressed delivered,    addressed beacons read at least once by their destination / addressed beacons sent.
    unique
  Addressed read again    copies read again because they arrived after the 90 s / 48-packet repeat check had
    (dup leak)            forgotten the packet, per addressed beacon (duplicates passed to the application).
  Peers in cluster        peer-table size, sampled every 1 s: mean over time and nodes, and the max.
  Cluster full            beacons from a new peer rejected because the table already held MAX_PEERS.
  Peers added / removed   peer-table turnover (removed = 30 s without a beacon heard directly).

4. INTERPRETATION
==========================================================================================================================

No back-off (B = 0) fails the spec: 84.2 ± 4.9 % PDR (worst receiver 78 %). Every node whose slot fell due while a
frame was on air sees the channel free at the same instant when it ends and transmits within the same 1 ms loop
pass, so those nodes cannot hear each other (half duplex) and the other receivers lock onto one of them and lose
the rest. Most of these losses are silent (2.5 CRC errors per node per hour against ~16 % of frames lost): the
SNR-capture rule (1 dB) usually lets one frame through.

Any back-off fixes this in the model: 99.86 % at 1.5 s, 99.86 % at 3 s, 99.92 % at 6 s and 99.97 % at 12 s. The
differences between 1.5, 3 and 6 s are inside the confidence intervals; 12 s is marginally better. The cost
grows linearly with B: mean channel access delay 3.2 s (1.5 s), 5.8 s (3 s), 11.7 s (6 s), 23.0 s (12 s), worst
case 29 / 48 / 93 / 181 s, time between transmissions 19.7 / 22.3 / 28.2 / 39.5 s, own beacons every 35 / 41 /
52 / 71 s (forwards take ~45 % of the slots, as in SM/O1), and the two-hop delay of forwarded copies 12.8 / 15.4 /
23.4 / 37.6 s. With 6 s and 12 s some forwards arrive after the 90 s repeat window (0.04 and 0.41 duplicate
reads per addressed packet). Mean peers held fall from 2.6 to 2.1 as beacons get rarer.

Best trade-off. With carrier sense as modelled (instant), the smallest back-off tried, 1.5 s, gives the best
PDR/delay trade-off: the same PDR as 3 s and 6 s (99.86 vs 99.92 %, not significant) at 27 % of the 6 s access
delay (3.2 vs 11.7 s) and 35 s instead of 52 s between own beacons (closer to the 30 s PEER_TIMEOUT).
3 s is equally good on PDR at half the 6 s delay.

The sensitivity run reverses the ranking. If the SX1278 needs ~4 preamble symbols before carrier sense sees a
frame, two nodes whose back-offs end within 131 ms both transmit, and a longer back-off spreads the end times:
PDR 83.1 % (0), 82.3 % (1.5 s), 84.4 % (3 s), 88.1 % (6 s), 92.2 % (12 s), with CRC errors highest at 1.5 s
(16.6 per node per hour). None reaches 95 % at 10 nodes; 12 s comes closest at about twice the 6 s delay.

How 6 s compares. The firmware's 6 s is a robust middle choice: within 0.1 point of the best PDR when sensing is
instant (but with 3.7x the access delay of 1.5 s), and 5.8 points better than 1.5 s when sensing is slow (but 4
points behind 12 s). Which back-off is best therefore depends on the real preamble-detection time of the node,
which these tests did not measure: if it is a symbol or two, 1.5-3 s is better than 6 s; if it is several
symbols, 6 s or more is needed and the back-off alone cannot hold 95 % PDR at 10 nodes. A bench test (two nodes
forced to start within a few ms to a few hundred ms of each other) would settle it. Keeping 6 s until then is
reasonable; shortening it should wait for that measurement.


5. LIMITATIONS
==========================================================================================================================
  * Calibration from one pair of nodes, at one site, on one day: the UCT rugby field, antennas at ground level
    (~1 m, carried), 420 usable beacons in one direction. Other terrain, antenna heights (e.g. on a tuk-tuk roof),
    vehicles and people around the nodes will change alpha, the reference loss and sigma.
  * Asymmetric links: Alice -> Bob delivered 3.3 % against 38.5 % for Bob -> Alice, so only Bob -> Alice was used
    and the model stands for a healthy node pair. If Alice's fault (likely its transmit path or antenna) exists
    on other units, real PDR will be lower. The two nodes also ran different beacon intervals in that test (3.5 s
    and 16.5 s).
  * Model shape: a single log-distance slope with independent log-normal shadowing cannot follow the measured
    plateau (25-40 % PDR from 250 to 325 m, then 0 of 20 beyond 325 m): the model is too optimistic at 100-200 m
    (by up to 30 points per bin) and too pessimistic at 275-325 m. The field allowed only 332 m, so the measured
    324 m is a lower bound on the range.
  * Shadowing is drawn independently for every frame (INET LogNormalShadowing), with no spatial or temporal
    correlation: in the model a weak link loses random frames, whereas a real obstructed link stays bad. sigma was
    fitted under the same independence assumption.
  * FLoRa's SNIR is not capped (a real SX127x saturates near +10 dB) and uses -137 dBm as noise, so rxSnr reads
    ~20 dB above the node's SNR. It does not affect reception decisions (sensitivity + 1 dB capture), and SNR is
    not a sweep metric.
  * Buildings do not attenuate (the physicalEnvironment is only drawn). Irrelevant to these open-area clusters, but
    the calibrated open-field channel now also applies to the SUMO/Erlangen Tvv configs, where it ignores
    buildings.
  * Carrier sense: in the baseline a frame counts as "being received" the moment it arrives above sensitivity;
    the SX127x needs some preamble symbols first. This is the largest uncertainty in the PDR results (99.6-99.9 %
    with instant sensing vs 72-93 % with 131 ms): the real figure lies between, and the detection time should be
    measured.
  * Collision model: FLoRa's (unchanged): same-SF capture at 1 dB, a frame is lost if overlapped (after its first
    2 preamble symbols) by one not 1 dB weaker; the receiver stays locked on the first frame. No partial-SINR or
    symbol-level model, no frequency-error effects (the measured frequency error is not modelled).
  * Traffic: the 90 % broadcast / 10 % addressed mix with uniformly random destinations is the report's assumption
    (no fleet data). Every node is saturated (always has a beacon due), as in the firmware. Host messages, SD-card
    loop delays (pollInterval covers only the 1 ms loop) and GPS payload are not modelled.
  * Stationary nodes in a 25 m disc: no mobility, so peer timeouts are caused only by the beacon interval, not by
    nodes driving out of range; real clusters change membership as tuk-tuks move.
  * Statistics: PDR from seq gaps does not count frames lost before the first or after the last frame heard from a
    sender (negligible over 3300 s), and a sender never heard at all would not count (did not happen: every node
    heard every other). The 25.5 ms processing offset is a constant from 10 PPS-timed indoor packets.
  * Validation: the two-node sweep uses the firmware's 15 s slot, so half-duplex overlaps are rare; the field run had
    Bob at a 3.5 s interval, and its overlap losses were removed before fitting.
